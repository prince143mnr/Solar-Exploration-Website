ANDROMEDA

The Andromeda Galaxy (Messier 31) is a barred spiral galaxy approximately 2.5 million light-years from Earth and the largest constituent of the Local Group of galaxies.

DISCOVERY & HISTORIC CHRONOLOGY

Observed by Persian astronomer Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi in 964 CE as a "little cloud". In 1924, Edwin Hubble resolved Cepheid variable stars in M31 using the Mount Wilson 100-inch Hooker telescope, conclusively proving that spiral nebulae were external "island universes" beyond the Milky Way.

In approximately 4.5 billion years, Andromeda and the Milky Way will engage in a gravitational merger. Because interstellar distances are so vast, individual stars will virtually never collide, but the galaxies will coalesce into a giant elliptical galaxy dubbed "Milkomeda".
